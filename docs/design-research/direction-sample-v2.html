Intellia AI Watchlist

Thursday 15 October 2026

Gemini 4 Argon’s pricing, scheduled agents out of preview in Claude Code, and a breach that OpenAI is describing very carefully.

Today’s digest

What changed today is price, not benchmarks: Google’s Gemini 4 Argon went general at 06:00, and the Flash tier is listed at half of what Gemini 3.5 cost per million output tokens, with a two-million-token context on every plan. For the engagements where we have been recommending a single-vendor stack, that number alone reopens the conversation.

Anthropic’s answer, quietly, was in Claude Code. Scheduled routines left research preview overnight, so a repository can now run an agent on a cron, open a pull request and post the diff to Slack with nobody at the keyboard. Two of our clients asked for exactly this in September. The catch is in the docs: a routine runs with the permissions of whoever created it, which makes it a governance question before it is a tooling one.

The security story is getting worse in a specific way. Three labs co-signed a letter warning that the pace of releases makes a serious model-weights breach inevitable within eighteen months, and OpenAI confirmed that the insider Fireship covers below had access to training infrastructure. Expect clients to ask about data residency and weight custody this week; the honest answer is that nobody buying an API has either.

In open source, the repo to watch is dossier-rs, a small Rust tool that reconstructs a client-side app’s source from shipped bundles and source maps well enough to run it. Fireship calls it “every app is now open source”, which overstates it, but it is the first of these that works on obfuscated React Native builds.

Nothing from Meta AI or Mistral earned a paragraph; both are in lab news below, and the Llama 5 licence thread on Hacker News is the one to read before any procurement call.
